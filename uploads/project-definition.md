# Yosh Iste'dodlar Kompasi — what this project is

The single source of truth for what we are building, who it is for, and what we
refuse to do. Everything downstream — landing page, visual identity, copy,
pricing, channel — should be derivable from this file. If a design decision
cannot be justified from something written here, either the design is wrong or
this file is incomplete.

Design-neutral on purpose. It describes the product and the person, not the
palette. The visual direction is the next document, and it starts from here.

---

## 1. In one sentence

**A parent sits with their 6–11 year old for half an hour, reads them questions
from a phone, types back what the child says — and ends up with a written
portrait of that child's strengths, interests and way of learning, built
entirely out of the child's own answers.**

The parent does not hand the phone to the child. The parent reads aloud, the
child talks, the parent types. That is the product's physical shape and it
matters more than any feature: **it is an evening spent with your child that
happens to produce a document**, not a test administered to a child.

## 2. The one feeling

A parent should finish and think: *"I learned something true about my child
tonight, and I didn't know it this morning."*

Not "I got a score." Not "I was told what's wrong." Something specific,
recognisable, and slightly surprising — the kind of observation a very
attentive teacher might make after a term, arriving after thirty minutes.

---

## 3. Who this is for — the ideal client

### The person

A parent in Uzbekistan, **28–42**, with at least one child in **grades 1–4**.
Usually the mother in practice for primary-school matters; the father is often
involved in the spending decision. Urban and semi-urban — Tashkent first, then
Samarkand, Bukhara, Namangan, Andijan, Fergana.

**On a phone, almost always.** Telegram is their default messaging app and they
live in it. Desktop is a minority case and mostly older or office-based users.

They speak **Uzbek (Latin script)**. Russian is often a second language but is
not the language of this product.

Income: enough that **49 000 so'm is a considered purchase, not a painful one** —
roughly a couple of taxi rides, far less than a month of a to'garak. They will
not buy it carelessly, but the price is not the obstacle. Trust is.

### What they believe

- **Education is the main lever on a child's future.** This is culturally deep
  and not something the page has to argue for.
- **To'garaklar are normal and expected.** Choosing the right one is an active
  family decision, often discussed with grandparents.
- **School grades do not tell them what they want to know.** A "5" in maths does
  not answer "what is my child actually like, and what should we do about it".
- **Talent has a window.** A widespread belief that ability must be spotted
  early — which is where the quiet urgency in this market comes from. We should
  not manufacture that urgency; it is already there and exploiting it would be
  ugly.

### The question actually in their head

> *"Is my child good at something — and am I missing it?"*

Everything else is downstream of that sentence. It contains both the hope and
the guilt, which is why it works.

### What they are afraid of

| Fear | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| Spending years and money on the wrong to'garak | The recommendation must be *reasoned*, tied to what the child said |
| Being told something is wrong with their child | Never diagnose. Open with strengths, always |
| A label being stuck on their child | Learning style is a *preference*, not a type. Levels are not rankings |
| Being judged as a parent | The tone is a colleague's, never an examiner's |
| "AI will just make something up" | The report quotes the child. The engagement gate makes a fabricated report impossible |
| Not having 30 free minutes | Pausing and resuming must be obvious and safe |

### What earns their trust

1. **It comes from a real education centre**, not a faceless app.
2. **The report is unmistakably about *their* child** — it quotes them.
3. **It refuses things.** Saying "this is not a diagnosis, not an IQ test, and
   we never compare your child to others" buys more trust than any claim.
4. **They were in the room.** They read the questions, they heard the answers,
   they typed them. The output cannot feel like a black box, because they fed it.

### Who this is NOT for

Saying this out loud sharpens everything:

- Parents seeking a clinical or psychological **diagnosis**.
- Parents of **teenagers** — the question bank is built for 6–11.
- Anyone who wants a **number, a score or a percentile**.
- Anyone expecting the **child to use the app alone**.
- Parents who want to be told their child is gifted. We report what we saw.

### Secondary audience

**Teachers.** The session is designed to be run by "an adult — a teacher or a
parent", and a teacher running it for a pupil is a legitimate and valuable use.
The landing speaks to parents; the product does not exclude teachers.

## 4. What we ask of them

This is the honest accounting, because the landing page has to earn all three:

1. **A Telegram login.** One tap of "Start" in a bot. Small in effort, real in
   commitment — that chat is personal.
2. **Thirty to forty minutes of an evening, with their child.** Not thirty
   minutes of scrolling. This is the large ask and the one most often
   underestimated.
3. **49 000 so'm**, later, to unlock the full report.

Most products sell a click. **This one sells an evening.**

## 5. What actually happens

```
Landing
   ↓
Telegram login          one tap in the bot; the bot may ask for the phone
   ↓                    with a Share-contact button (verified, no SMS code)
Child details           name or nickname, grade 1–4, optional age / goal / notes
   ↓                    + the adult's confirmation that they are the parent or teacher
The session             ~30–40 min. The AI asks 2–4 questions at a time and
   ↓                    switches between directions so the child does not tire.
                        Adult reads aloud → child answers → adult types.
                        "Skip", "simpler question" and "pause" always available.
Report                  generated from the session's real answers only
   ↓
Free part               the strengths portrait + the letter to the child
Paid part               the other eight sections — 49 000 so'm, once, link is permanent
```

Everything is resumable. The session link works from any device; a parent can
stop at bedtime and finish tomorrow.

## 6. What the session looks at

Three assessment directions, seven lenses. The parent never needs to know a
single technical term — **the names below are for us, not for them.**

### The three directions

| Direction | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Mantiq va tafakkur** | Patterns and sequences, arithmetic reasoning, spatial puzzles, memory games, language tasks (making up a story, rhyme, analogy) |
| **Psixologiya va qiziqishlar** | Interests, attitude to mistakes, self-regulation, emotions, preferred way of learning, curiosity and dreams |
| **Tana va harakat** | Energy level, favourite games, simple physical mini-tasks the adult observes (balance, catching, jumping, rhythm) |

Each direction is closed only once enough evidence exists — e.g. logic needs 5–6
tasks of at least 3 kinds. Coverage is a requirement, not a suggestion.

### The seven lenses

1. **Strengths** — three things this child does best, each with an example from
   their own answer.
2. **Where they stand** — per direction: *Shakllanmoqda / Me'yorda / Kuchli*
   (forming / on track / strong), against what is typical for their own grade
   group. **Never against other children.**
3. **Talents map** — Gardner's eight intelligences, written in plain Uzbek:
   words, logic and numbers, imagination and shape, music, movement, getting on
   with people, understanding oneself, noticing nature. Only what was actually
   observed is listed.
4. **Interests compass** — RIASEC leaning: builder, researcher, creator, helper,
   organiser. Presented as a *current* leaning, not a destiny.
5. **Attitude to mistakes and self-regulation** — Dweck's growth mindset:
   what the child does when something goes wrong; persistence, attention,
   patience. Tied to specific moments, never labelled.
6. **Emotional intelligence** — recognising and naming feelings, managing them,
   empathy, peer relationships. At this age this is *forming*, and the report
   says so as a growth point, not a deficiency.
7. **Learning style** — whether they got there faster by hearing, seeing or
   doing. Mostly *observed during the session* rather than asked about, so it
   costs no extra time. Explicitly a preference: the report is forbidden from
   saying "a visual child".

### Age groups

**A = grades 1–2** (~6–8), **B = grades 3–4** (~8–11). Questions adapt; the bank
is a template of task *types and difficulty*, never a fixed script — every
session generates fresh variants with local names, foods and games.

## 7. What comes out — the report, in ten parts

| # | Part | Free? |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Strengths portrait | **Free** |
| 02 | Where they stand | paid |
| 03 | Talents map | paid |
| 04 | Interests compass | paid |
| 05 | Attitude to mistakes | paid |
| 06 | Emotional intelligence | paid |
| 07 | Learning style | paid |
| 08 | 3–6 month roadmap | paid |
| 09 | Sport and club recommendations | paid |
| 10 | A letter to the child, to be read aloud | **Free** |

**Why 01 and 10 are free.** The child did the work. Holding all of it hostage
would be a poor trade for them, and the letter is *addressed to the child* —
charging their parent to read it to them is not something this product does. The
free part is also what proves the report is real and specific, which is what
makes the rest worth buying.

**The letter is the emotional core of the entire product.** A parent reading
their own child a sentence like *"you are not afraid of hard questions — that is
a real strength"* is the moment this justifies itself. Every design decision
should protect that moment.

## 8. What we refuse to do

This is not a compliance section. **It is the product's identity**, and most of
it is already enforced in code.

- **No diagnosis.** No medical or psychological conclusion, ever.
- **No IQ score, no number, no percentile.** Only observations in words.
- **No comparison between children.** Not in the report, not anywhere. Levels
  describe a child against typical expectations for their own grade group.
- **No fabricated observations.** A report cannot be generated until the child
  has actually answered — the server refuses. If a child barely engaged, the
  report says so honestly and suggests running a proper session instead.
- **No labels.** "A visual child", "a shy child" — forbidden phrasing.
- **Minimal data about the child.** Name or nickname, grade, optional age. There
  is *no database column* for surname, address, school or photograph. Goal and
  notes stay on our server and are never sent to the AI.
- **No invented social proof.** No stock testimonials, no borrowed logos, no
  user counts we do not have. When we have real numbers we will use them.
- **No urgency theatre.** No countdowns, no false scarcity. The parental anxiety
  in this market is real and pre-existing; amplifying it would be ugly.
- **The child never holds the phone.**
- **A working opt-out.** `/stop` in the bot silences everything automated. The
  report they paid for still arrives — that is not marketing.

## 9. Money

| | |
|---|---|
| The session | **Free** |
| Report parts 01 and 10 | **Free** |
| The other eight parts | **49 000 so'm**, one payment, permanent link |
| Payment rail | **Payme** (card). Card details never touch our server |

A second revenue path exists and is not in conflict: the centre offers courses
matched to the report's recommendations. That offer is optional and the report
is the parent's regardless.

The price is stated **before the parent spends thirty minutes**, never at the
paywall. A parent who discovers the cost after doing the work has been misled by
us.

## 10. Telegram, and why it is not optional

Telegram is the spine of the product, not a login convenience:

- **It is how the report comes back.** Browser closed, phone changed — the link
  survives in a chat they already keep.
- **It is the only channel we are given permission to use.** A phone number
  typed into a form is a number you may call. A chat the person started
  themselves is standing permission to message. That distinction is why the
  login exists at all.
- **It carries the follow-ups**: the report, and a reminder if they abandon the
  session, never bought the report, or logged in and never started. One message
  per person per situation, ever.
- **The phone arrives verified** through Telegram's Share-contact button — no
  SMS codes, no typos.

**It is also the product's biggest leak** (see §11). The step is small; the
problem is that it arrives unannounced.

## 11. What we already know — measured, not assumed

Real drop-off from the live funnel, 30-day window:

| Step | Reaching it | Lost |
|---|---|---|
| Opened the site | 420 | — |
| Went to the start page | 250 | **−170 (40%)** |
| Pressed "log in with Telegram" | 205 | −45 (18%) |
| **Completed the Telegram login** | 96 | **−109 (53%)** |
| Started the session | 81 | −7 |
| Child gave a first answer | 64 | −17 |
| Report produced | 48 | −16 |
| Opened the report | 44 | −4 |
| **Bought it** | 17 | −27 |

Two numbers govern everything:

- **40% never leave the landing page.** The next step is not being made to feel
  worth taking.
- **53% start the Telegram login and never finish it.** The single most fixable
  leak in the product. People abandon it because it is a surprise, not because
  it is hard.

This is instrumented continuously, so design work here is judged on evidence
rather than taste. Targets: landing→start under 25% loss, login completion
above 70%.

## 12. Voice

- **Uzbek, Latin script.** Runs 15–20% longer than English — every headline must
  be designed at its longest plausible length.
- **A knowledgeable colleague, never an examiner.** We are a second pair of eyes
  on a child the parent knows far better than we do.
- **Concrete over clever.** "Xato javobdan keyin yana urindi" beats any adjective.
- **Warm about the child, plain with the adult.** Questions read aloud are
  playful and simple. Text for the parent is direct.
- **Never anxious.** No pressure, no guilt, no implication that they have
  already left it too late.

## 13. What a parent does instead of us

Honest competitive picture — mostly not software:

| Alternative | Why they might | Where it falls short |
|---|---|---|
| Ask the class teacher | Free, trusted, knows the child | One perspective, academic only, rarely specific about interests |
| Try a to'garak and see | Concrete, social | Months and money per guess |
| Online "IQ tests for kids" | Instant, cheap | A number, no recommendations, often nonsense |
| A private psychologist | Thorough, credible | Expensive, slow, feels clinical — most parents do not want a diagnosis |
| Do nothing | Free | The default, and our real competitor |

**Our actual competitor is inertia.** Not another product — an evening that
passes like every other evening.

## 14. What the landing page must achieve

Design-agnostic goals, in priority order:

1. **Make the Telegram step feel small and expected** before anyone meets it.
   This is the 53%.
2. **Sell the evening, not the click.** A parent must picture themselves doing
   this tonight, with their child, and want to.
3. **Prove the report is real and specific** — showing it beats describing it.
4. **State the price before the work**, plainly.
5. **Be true in every claim.** Nothing free that is not free; no proof we do not
   have; no ranking language.
6. **Work at 360px on a mid-range Android at 21:00.** Mobile is the product.
7. **Protect the letter.** The most emotional thing we have should not be
   buried among features.

### The current visual system (context, not a constraint)

Today the whole app uses a "Modernist" design system: near-white `#f3f2f2`,
near-black ink, a single hot red `#ec3013`, very heavy display type, hard 2px
rules and square corners.

**This is what we are moving away from.** Two things worth knowing before
choosing a new direction:

- The system is used by **every screen** — landing, setup, session, report,
  admin. Re-colouring only the landing would leave a parent crossing a visible
  seam at exactly the moment we ask them to commit. Either the pivot covers the
  product, or the landing must stay recognisably related to it.
- The red is doing real work in the report (levels, accents). A new palette
  needs an answer for "how does a strength look" that is not alarm-coloured.

## 15. Open decisions

- **Scope of the visual pivot**: landing only, or the whole product?
- **Whether the sale is the report or the courses.** Currently both. The funnel
  measures them separately for exactly this reason.
- **Social proof**: nothing may be invented, so the first real numbers or a
  genuine parent quote would be the single biggest available trust upgrade.
- **Teachers** as an addressed audience, or left as an unadvertised use.
