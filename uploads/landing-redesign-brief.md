# Landing page redesign — designer brief

**Product:** Yosh Iste'dodlar Kompasi — an AI-guided talent-orientation session a
parent runs with their 7–10 year old, ending in a personalised report.
**Deliverable:** a deep, scroll-driven landing page that turns a cold visitor
into a parent who logs in and starts the session tonight.
**Reference for mechanics only:** deepstash.com. Take the *engine*, not the look.
Copying its aesthetic, mascot, dark theme or testimonial pattern is a fail.

---

## 1. The one thing this page must do

Not "get a click". The page is asking a parent for three escalating things, and
it has to earn all three before they leave:

1. **Their Telegram identity** — they log in through a bot, which is a real
   commitment in a country where that chat is personal.
2. **Thirty minutes of an evening, with their child** — not thirty minutes of
   scrolling. They read questions aloud, the child answers, they type it in.
3. **Later, ~49 000 so'm** for the full report.

Most landing pages sell a click. This one sells an *evening*. Design for that.

## 2. Who is reading it

A parent in Uzbekistan, 28–45, of a child in grades 1–4. Overwhelmingly on a
**phone**, often at night, often with the child nearby. Uzbek (Latin script).
Not a specialist — they will not know what "Gardner" or "RIASEC" means and must
never need to. They are not sceptical of AI so much as tired of being sold
things about their child.

Their actual, unglamorous question: *"Is my child good at something, and am I
missing it?"*

## 3. What we know is broken (use these as the design targets)

The current page is instrumented. Real drop-off, 30-day window:

| Step | Reaching it | Lost |
|---|---|---|
| Opened the site | 420 | — |
| Went to the start page | 250 | **−170 (40%)** |
| Pressed "log in with Telegram" | 205 | −45 (18%) |
| **Finished the Telegram login** | 96 | **−109 (53%)** |
| Started the session | 81 | −7 |
| Report produced | 48 | −33 |

Two numbers matter:

- **40% never leave the landing page.** The page does not make the next step
  feel worth taking.
- **53% press the login button and never come back.** They were not told the
  step was coming, did not understand why, or lost their nerve inside Telegram.

**The second is the brief's hardest requirement.** The Telegram step must be
*pre-sold on the landing page* — explained, justified, made to feel small —
rather than sprung on them one screen later. Hiding it to protect the click-rate
is exactly what is failing now.

**Success = those two numbers move.** Target: landing→start under 25% loss,
login completion above 70%. It is measured weekly in the admin panel, so the
design will be judged on evidence, not taste.

## 4. What to take from the reference

deepstash earns the scroll. Steal the mechanics:

- **Benefit cascade.** A sequence of sections, each doing one psychological job,
  each framed as a change in the reader rather than a feature of the product.
- **Emotional first, rational second.** Feeling → proof → mechanics → price.
  Never open with how it works.
- **Motion as the reward for scrolling.** Something advances as you go, so
  continuing feels productive rather than long.
- **An abundance cue.** They fan out 50+ book covers to say "there is a lot
  here". We have an honest equivalent — see §6.
- **CTAs at different intent levels**, repeated, never identical in wording.

## 5. What to reject from it — non-negotiable

- **No invented social proof.** No stock-photo testimonials, no "Sarah J.", no
  "10M+ users", no logo wall, no star ratings. We have no user numbers yet and
  the project forbids faking them. If you need trust in a slot, use a true
  statement about the method. This rule is absolute; a comp with placeholder
  testimonials will be rejected.
- **No dark theme, no mascot, no illustration-led whimsy.** Wrong register for a
  parent making a decision about their child.
- **Do not hide the product.** deepstash shows no screenshots so you must
  download. We do the opposite: the report *is* the product, it is specific and
  credible, and showing it is our strongest asset.
- **No urgency theatre.** No countdowns, no "only today", no fake scarcity.
- **No comparison between children, ever.** No percentiles, no "better than
  80%", no IQ framing. The product's ethical core is that children are not
  ranked against each other. Copy or visuals implying it break the product.

## 6. The material you have to work with

The assessment looks at seven things through the child's own answers. This is
the honest abundance cue — the page currently advertises three:

1. Strengths portrait
2. Where they stand across thinking / psychology / movement
3. **Talents map** (eight kinds of intelligence — name them in plain Uzbek)
4. **Interests compass** (builder / researcher / creator / helper / organiser…)
5. **Growth mindset and self-regulation** — how they handle a mistake
6. **Emotional intelligence** — naming and managing feelings
7. **Learning style** — do they learn faster by hearing, seeing or doing

Plus: a 3–6 month plan, sport and club recommendations, and a short letter
written to be read aloud to the child.

That last one is the emotional payload of the whole product. A parent reading
their own child a sentence like *"you are not afraid of hard questions — that is
a real strength"* is the moment this product justifies itself. Find the place in
the scroll where that lands hardest.

## 7. Suggested scroll narrative

Yours to restructure — these are the *jobs*, not a layout.

| # | Job | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero.** Name the outcome in one breath. | Must carry: what it is, that it is done *together* with the child, ~30 min, session free. Not a feature list. |
| 2 | **Recognition.** "That is my thought." | Four real parent sentences already exist and test well. Keep their honesty. |
| 3 | **The shift.** From guessing to seeing. | The turn: school grades measure one narrow thing; this looks at seven. |
| 4 | **What is actually looked at.** | The seven lenses. The abundance moment. Plain Uzbek, no jargon. |
| 5 | **The report, revealed not described.** | The scroll centrepiece — see §8. |
| 6 | **The letter to the child.** | Give it its own full-bleed moment. Quietest section, biggest feeling. |
| 7 | **How it works — four steps, Telegram first.** | Explicitly de-risk the login: why it exists (the report comes back there), how long (one tap), what we do not get. **This section carries the 53%.** |
| 8 | **What this is NOT.** | Not a diagnosis, not an IQ test, no ranking, no data sold. Counter-intuitively the strongest trust section, because it is all true. |
| 9 | **Price, plainly.** | Session free · first part of the report free · full analysis 49 000 so'm, one payment, link works forever. Stated before they invest 30 minutes — never at the paywall. |
| 10 | **Objections.** | Short. Cost, age range, "is AI judging my child", data, time. |
| 11 | **Close.** | Restate the evening, not the click. |

## 8. The scroll device — the thing that makes it "deep"

One connective idea, yours to invent, but here is the shape worth beating:

**The report assembles itself as the reader descends.** It enters early as an
almost-empty sheet with the child's name. Each section the reader passes adds a
real line to it — a strength, a talent, a learning-style note, finally the
letter. By the bottom they have watched a blank page become something specific
about a child, which is precisely what the product does.

This turns scroll depth into the argument itself rather than a container for it,
and it converts the reference's "keep scrolling" prompt into something that
earns the scroll.

Constraints: must degrade to a clean static stack on low-end Android and with
reduced-motion on; must never block reading; must work at 360px wide.

## 9. Visual constraints

Existing design system, already built ("Modernist") — work inside it:

- Background `#f3f2f2`, ink `#201e1d`, single accent `#ec3013`
- Heavy display type (800 weight), tight tracking, large clamp-scaled headings
- Hard 2px rules and borders, square corners, generous whitespace
- Small uppercase labels for section eyebrows
- No shadows-as-decoration, no gradients, no rounded pill aesthetics

Deviate only with a stated reason. If the concept needs one new element (a
motion pattern, a card treatment), propose it as an addition to the system.

**Uzbek runs ~15–20% longer than English.** Design headlines at the longest
plausible string, not the shortest. Test every breakpoint at 360px.

## 10. Deliverables

1. Mobile comps first (360 / 390), then desktop (1440). Mobile is the product.
2. Full scroll, every section, both configurations of §9's price block (a paid
   build and a free build — both ship).
3. A short motion spec for the §8 device: what moves, on what trigger, and the
   reduced-motion fallback.
4. Uzbek copy in the comps. Lorem ipsum hides exactly the length problems that
   break this layout.
5. One paragraph on why your structure beats the one in §7 where it differs.

## 11. How this will be judged

In order:

1. Does the Telegram step feel small and reasonable **before** the reader
   reaches it?
2. Would a tired parent at 21:00 on a phone still be scrolling at section 6?
3. Is every claim on the page true? (Nothing free that is not free, no invented
   proof, no ranking language.)
4. Does it look like this product and not like a SaaS template?
5. Do the two funnel numbers in §3 move within a month of launch?
